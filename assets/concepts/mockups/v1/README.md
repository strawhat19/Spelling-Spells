The HTML is a standalone editable mockup with local sample data; it is not a scaffolded or published app. Listen controls demonstrate the pronunciation text rather than claiming recorded audio exists.

## Content direction

The hero introduces the collection and makes a spell record understandable before requiring navigation. Public browsing is the default. A record can cover:

- Canonical spelling, aliases, letter-by-letter spelling, and optional original-script forms.
- Pronunciation aids, syllables, and a sourced pronunciation/audio reference when available.
- Definition, effect, usage, counterspell, and limitations in the fictional setting.
- Word breakdown, language, and etymology, with confirmed origins distinguished from suggested interpretations.
- Universe, work, medium, and sources; a dragon shout remains labelled as a shout rather than being flattened into a generic charm.
- Danger, utility, and required skill, presented as editorial catalog judgments rather than official cross-universe statistics.

This concept does not invent collection counts, canon translations, or a universal magical scoring system. The example ratings below communicate the intended interface, not an authoritative ranking.

## Sample records and source notes

### Lumos

Harry Potter · Wand-Lighting Charm. Produces light at a wand's tip, with Nox as its counterspell. Letter tiles: **L U M O S**. **LOO-mos** is a reader-friendly English pronunciation aid; **LU · MOS** is a syllable grouping, not a morpheme analysis. Suggested etymology connects the name to Latin *lumen*, meaning light. Editorial example: danger **1/5**, utility **5/5**, skill **Beginner**.

Sources: [Official spell encyclopedia](https://www.harrypotter.com/fact-file/spells/the-wand-lighting-charm), [official discussion of spell etymology](https://www.harrypotter.com/features/the-etymology-of-harry-potter-spells). The etymology article uses tentative language, so the interface labels this **Suggested Origin**.

### Fus Ro Dah

The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim · Dragon Shout · Unrelenting Force. A forceful shout that pushes opponents away. Spelling is presented as three words: **F U S / R O / D A H**. The game performance is the intended pronunciation reference. This round does not claim an independently sourced literal translation for each word. Editorial example: danger **4/5**, utility **4/5**, skill **Advanced**.

Sources: [Bethesda's Dragon Language development article](https://elderscrolls.bethesda.net/en-AU/news/1rL4bCScN6RYXaXQb4ownj/how-bethesda-game-studios-made-skyrims-dragon-language), [Bethesda's memorable Skyrim moments](https://elderscrolls.bethesda.net/zh-TW/news/7-memorable-moments-from-the-elder-scrolls-v-skyrim). Language inspiration should not be presented as a proven etymology of this individual phrase.

### Brisingr

Inheritance Cycle · Ancient Language. A word of power meaning **fire**. Letter tiles: **B R I S I N G R**. Official pronunciation: **BRISS-ing-gur**. Christopher Paolini describes Old Norse inspiration for the word. Editorial example: danger **4/5**, utility **4/5**, skill **Intermediate**.

Sources: [Paolini's Ancient Language guide](https://www.paolini.net/fans/invented-languages-inheritance-cycle/ancient-language/), [author FAQ](https://www.paolini.net/faq/), [official pronunciation text](https://www.paolini.net/2018/06/11/man-arms-brisingr/), [official pronunciation audio index](https://www.paolini.net/audio-pronunciation-guide/things/).
